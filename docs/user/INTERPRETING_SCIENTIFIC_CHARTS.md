# NASA-FLAME: Interpreting Scientific Charts

## 1. Radial Temperature Profiles ($T(r)$)

In the **TemperatureDistributionChart** under the **Radial $T(r)$** tab, you observe temperature decaying from the core to ambient air:

### Key Features to Look For:
1. **The Peak Reaction Zone ($r_f$)**: The steep peak represents the stoichiometric sheet where fuel and oxygen meet in exact proportions. For a $4\text{ mm}$ burner, this typically occurs at $r \approx 5.0 - 7.0\text{ mm}$.
2. **Inner Core ($r < r_f$)**: Governed by endothermic fuel pyrolysis and vaporization. Temperatures remain lower ($800 - 1200\text{ K}$) before rising sharply into the reaction front.
3. **Outer Thermal Boundary ($r > r_f$)**: In microgravity, the absence of convective sweeping causes heat to conduct outward into a very thick spherical thermal envelope, cooling gradually toward ambient $298\text{ K}$.

## 2. Orthographic Geometry & Aspect Ratios ($L/W$)

In the **FlameGeometryViewer**, two cross-sections are presented side-by-side:

### Microgravity ($0g$):
- **Aspect Ratio ($L/W$)**: Approaches $1.0 \pm 0.05$.
- **Soot Zone**: Solid soot particles do not rise into a plume; instead, they slowly drift radially outward, forming an enclosing spherical soot shell around the reaction front.
- **Coloration**: Homogeneous blue chemiluminescent glow ($\text{CH}^*$ and $\text{C}_2^*$) due to low soot residence temperature.

### Terrestrial ($1g$):
- **Aspect Ratio ($L/W$)**: Typically $3.0 - 4.5$.
- **Soot Zone**: Strong buoyant acceleration drives hot carbon soot particles vertically into the apex tip, where they reach incandescence ($T > 1600\text{ K}$) and glow brilliant yellow-orange.

## 3. Fluid Dynamic Dimensionless Scaling Numbers

When interpreting the dimensionless metrics generated by the AI Engine and displayed on the **GravityComparisonChart**:

### 3.1 Grashof Number ($Gr$)
$$Gr = \frac{g \beta (T_f - T_\infty) L^3}{\nu^2}$$
- **$Gr \gg 10^4$ (Terrestrial $1g$)**: Buoyant forces completely overpower molecular viscous resistance, generating a rapid convective chimney draft.
- **$Gr \approx 0$ (Microgravity $0g$)**: Natural convection is physically non-existent.

### 3.2 Richardson Number ($Ri$)
$$Ri = \frac{Gr}{Re^2}$$
- **$Ri > 1.0$**: Natural buoyant convection dominates forced flow.
- **$Ri < 0.1$**: Forced convection or pure diffusion dominates.

### 3.3 Rayleigh Number ($Ra$)
$$Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$$
- Quantifies buoyancy-driven heat transfer. At $Ra \approx 0$, thermal energy is transported exclusively via Fourier heat conduction.
